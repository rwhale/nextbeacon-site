Last updated 28 October 2026. This page is written in plain language on purpose. If anything here is unclear, ask.

This website

This site collects nothing about who you are. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics or advertising trackers, and has no forms. One exception, so you know: links in NextBeacon's own social posts pass through a short counting address first, which records that the link was clicked (no name, no address, nothing else), and if you arrived that way your browser keeps that post's short tag for 30 days so that a purchase can be credited to the post. It never leaves your browser except as part of a purchase. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which keeps the ordinary server records any web host keeps, such as the address a page was requested from, under GitHub's privacy statement. The fonts load from Google Fonts, which means your browser requests two font files from Google when the page opens.

The NextBeacon program on your PC

NextBeacon keeps your résumé, your profile, your company list, your scores and your application history in files on your own PC, in its own folder. There is no NextBeacon server. Nothing you put into NextBeacon is sent to Rob Hale, and Rob Hale cannot see it.

The program does connect to other services, each under your own account and that service's own terms:

  • Anthropic (Claude). The AI work happens through your own Claude account. To score a posting or draft a résumé, NextBeacon sends the posting text and the relevant parts of your profile and résumé to Anthropic. What Anthropic does with that is governed by Anthropic's privacy policy and your plan.
  • Employers' job boards. NextBeacon reads the public postings on the career pages and job boards of the companies you choose. Reading a public page tells that site only what any visit tells it.
  • The daily report email. Each morning NextBeacon on your PC builds the report and hands it to the NextBeacon service, which sends it to the address you chose from reports@mynextbeacon.com. The report passes through the service and is not kept: the service records only that a report of a certain size went out, so it can stop a PC sending more than ten a day. Nothing to set up, and no email password of yours is ever asked for or stored.
  • The NextBeacon service. When you install, the installer sends your purchase key and a code that identifies your PC to a small NextBeacon service, to fetch your unlock code. Each morning NextBeacon asks the same service, with the same two things and the version it is running, whether your licence still stands and whether a newer version exists, and downloads that version if so. With that same morning check NextBeacon also sends usage counts tied to your purchase: how many postings it has scanned and scored, and, unless you switch it off on its Config page, how many you have marked applied, interviewing, offered and hired. Counts only: never a company name, a job title or a line of your résumé. Rob adds them up across all buyers, never quotes one person's, and uses the totals to describe NextBeacon.

When you buy

Payment is handled by Stripe. Your card details go to Stripe, never to NextBeacon. Rob receives your name, your email address and the purchase record from Stripe, and keeps them so he can deliver your copy, handle any refund and verify your licence.

Rob does not sell, rent or share your details with anyone. He may send buyers an occasional NextBeacon news email; a checkbox on NextBeacon's Config page switches that off, and the choice reaches him with the next morning check. If you want your purchase record deleted later, ask; it means your licence can no longer be verified.

What Rob never has

  • Your résumé, your profile or your company list.
  • Your scores, your applications or your pipeline.
  • Your Claude account or password, or any email password.
  • Your daily report. It passes through the NextBeacon service on its way to you and is not kept, and Rob does not read it.
  • Your card details.
